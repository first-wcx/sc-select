# Related work

## Text-to-image generation

Text-to-image generation has progressed from early generative adversarial networks to diffusion-based models. Denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPM) [ho2020ddpm] established the foundation for high-quality image synthesis. Latent diffusion models [rombach2022latent] moved the diffusion process to a compressed latent space, enabling efficient high-resolution generation and forming the backbone of Stable Diffusion. DALL-E 2 [ramesh2022hierarchical] and Imagen [saharia2022imagen] demonstrated that scaling text encoders with CLIP and T5 can significantly improve text-image alignment. More recently, SDXL [podell2024sdxl] introduced architectural improvements including dual text encoders and a refinement model. Transformer-based architectures have emerged as alternatives to U-Net, with PixArt-alpha [chen2023pixart] and PixArt-Sigma [chen2024pixartsigma] demonstrating efficient training of diffusion transformers. Stable Diffusion 3 [esser2024sd3] and FLUX.1 adopted rectified flow matching with multimodal diffusion transformers, achieving improved text rendering and prompt adherence. SC-Select does not modify these generators; it operates after generation by choosing from a candidate pool.

## Compositional text-to-image evaluation

Benchmarks such as GenEval [gokaslan2023geneval] and T2I-CompBench [huang2023t2icompbench] evaluate whether generated images satisfy object, attribute, relation, and counting requirements. GenEval decomposes prompts into object-level criteria and uses detector-based verification. T2I-CompBench covers attribute binding, spatial relations, and non-spatial relations with both automated and human evaluation metrics. GenAI-Bench [lin2024vqascore] introduces VQAScore, leveraging vision-language models for compositional assessment. These benchmarks motivate the core design of SC-Select: complex prompts should be evaluated at the level of fine-grained constraints rather than only by global caption-image similarity.

## Preference and reward models

ImageReward [li2023imagereward] learns human preferences from expert comparisons and can serve as a reward signal for RLHF-based diffusion model fine-tuning. HPSv2 [wu2023hpsv2] extends preference scoring with a larger-scale benchmark. PickScore [kirstain2023pickapic], trained on human preference choices from the Pick-a-Pic dataset, outperforms CLIP-based and aesthetic metrics in aligning with human judgments. These models are important baselines because they can select images that humans may prefer visually. However, preference scores may not explicitly diagnose which prompt constraints are satisfied or violated. SC-Select is complementary: it targets interpretable constraint satisfaction.

## Vision-language models as evaluators

Large vision-language models have made it practical to parse generated images into structured descriptions. CLIP [radford2021clip] established the foundation for vision-language alignment through contrastive pre-training on large-scale image-text pairs. LLaVA [liu2023llava] introduced visual instruction tuning by connecting CLIP encoders with language models, later refined in LLaVA-1.5 [liu2023llava15]. LLaVA-OneVision [li2024llavaonevision] extends this to multi-image and video scenarios. Qwen2.5-VL [qwen2025qwen25vl] achieves strong OCR and grounding capabilities with dynamic resolution processing. InternVL [chen2024internvl] scales vision-language modeling with progressive alignment. CogVLM [wang2024cogvlm] introduces visual expert parameters within each transformer layer for deep fusion. SC-Select relies on this capability to build perception graphs. This reliance also introduces a key limitation: parser errors can propagate into CSR scores.

## Explainable evaluation

Metrics such as VIEScore [ku2023viescore] and VQAScore [lin2024vqascore] reflect a broader movement toward instruction-aware and explainable evaluation of generated visual content. VIEScore leverages multimodal LLMs to assess semantic alignment, quality, and relevance. CLIPScore [hessel2021clipscore] provides reference-free evaluation through CLIP embedding similarity, though it struggles with compositional understanding. Traditional metrics like FID [heusel2017fid] measure distributional similarity but do not assess per-prompt adherence. SC-Select follows the explainable direction but uses the structured score not only for evaluation, but also for selecting the final output from multiple candidates.

## Consistency-based selection

The idea of using consistency across multiple samples to assess reliability has been explored in LLM hallucination detection. SelfCheckGPT [manakul2023selfcheckgpt] shows that statements present across multiple stochastic samples are more likely to be factual. SC-Select applies a related principle to image generation: by generating multiple candidates and checking each against explicit semantic contracts, the method identifies candidates that satisfy prompt constraints. This is distinct from best-of-N sampling in language models, where a single reward model scores candidates; SC-Select uses fine-grained, interpretable constraint satisfaction rates across five semantic dimensions.
